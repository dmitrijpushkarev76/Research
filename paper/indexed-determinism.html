Determinism Is an Indexed Predicate

A determinism verdict is not a function of a law. It is a function of a law and three further coordinates, each independently capable of flipping the answer — and the standing disputes in the field are disputes about coordinates, not about physics.

Abstract

The question is the world deterministic? is standardly treated as a single question with an unknown answer. It is not. Fixing a law L does not fix a determinism verdict: the verdict is additionally a function of three modelling coordinates, each of which has been debated in isolation as though it were the whole difficulty. σ fixes how states are individuated; δ fixes whether a history must be defined at every time or merely be maximal; μ fixes which models count as models of the theory. A fourth coordinate — the agreement relation itself — is already well mapped and is held fixed here.

The paper's contribution is three results and one diagnosis. First, an independence theorem: each of σ, δ, μ can reverse a verdict with the other two held fixed, so none is eliminable and no definition of determinism that suppresses any of them is well-formed. Second, σ is non-monotone in both directions: coarsening the state space can manufacture determinism and can destroy it, with explicit non-degenerate countermodels for each. The second direction is the one that matches the Ornstein–Weiss isomorphism results, and it is the direction usually stated backwards. Third, Everettian level-relativity is not a separate phenomenon: the branch projection is a σ-redescription, so "one history or many" at different levels is the same coordinate the hole argument turns on. All results are machine-checked.

The diagnosis is that the field's standing disputes sort cleanly onto the coordinates. Norton's dome is a μ-dispute; the hole argument is a σ-dispute; whether finite-time blow-up refutes determinism is a δ-dispute; Everettian branching is a σ-dispute. This explains a fact otherwise puzzling: why a century of additional mathematics has settled none of them. They are not mathematical questions. Applying the framework corrects one widely repeated claim — that the linearity of the Schrödinger equation immunises quantum mechanics against the pathologies of classical mechanics. It does not: failure of essential self-adjointness reproduces them exactly, and the classical and quantum failures are the same failure.

1The problem with the question

Here is a fact about the literature that ought to be more troubling than it is. The determinism of classical particle mechanics has been argued about continuously since 1986, with full agreement on the equations. The hole argument has been argued about since 1987, with full agreement on the field equations and on what a diffeomorphism is. Whether Everettian quantum mechanics describes one history or many has been argued about since 1957, with full agreement on the Schrödinger equation. In none of these cases is there a disputed calculation. In all of them the dispute persists.

The standard diagnosis is that these are interpretive questions, which is true but unilluminating: it names the residue without saying what the residue is made of. This paper gives a structural answer. The verdict "L is deterministic" is not a function of L. It is a function of L together with a triple of coordinates that must be fixed before the predicate denotes anything at all:

  • σ — individuation. Which redescription of the state space carries the physics? The hole argument is the question whether the metric-on-a-manifold or the isometry class is the state.
  • δ — temporal domain. Must a history be defined at every time, or need it only be maximal? Finite-time blow-up, escape to infinity and geodesic incompleteness all turn on this.
  • μ — admissibility. Which models count as models of the theory? Norton's dome is the question whether the dome is a Newtonian system.

A fourth coordinate, the agreement relation — whether worlds must agree at an instant or on a segment, in one temporal direction or two, qualitatively or haecceitistically — is the one the literature has already mapped: Montague and Lewis fixed it one way, Belot's D1–D3 discriminate several, and Müller and Placek [26] and Halvorson, Manchak and Weatherall [21] are recent full treatments. Precisely because it is well mapped, it is held fixed here, at the Montague/Lewis setting, so that the other three can be isolated. Everything below would need redoing at each other setting, which is itself part of the point.

Calling determinism "description-relative" is not new; Belot [6], Butterfield [7] and Werndl [34] all say something in the neighbourhood. What is new here is that the relativity has separable coordinates, that they are provably independent, and that the field's disputes sort onto them one-to-one. That sorting is the payoff: it predicts which disputes further mathematics can settle (none of these) and which it can (the analytic questions inside a fixed coordinate assignment).

2Framework

Everything is stated over discrete time for readability; nothing depends on that, and the analytic examples in §7 and §10 are continuous. The definitions are deliberately weak, so that no determinism thesis is smuggled into the vocabulary.

Definition 1 · History

A history is a partial function h : T → S, written ℕ → Option S. Partiality is the primitive. Taking total functions as the primitive — as the previous draft of this paper did, and as most treatments do silently — does not avoid the δ coordinate; it fixes it by stipulation, which is a different thing.

Definition 2 · Law

A law L is a predicate on histories. Not an evolution operator, not a transition function, not a differential equation: any of those builds the initial-value paradigm into the vocabulary before any physics is done, and would exclude the constraint-based laws of Adlam [1] by fiat.

Definition 3 · Agreement (the coordinate held fixed)

h₁ and h₂ meet iff they are both defined, and equal, at some one instant. This is the Montague/Lewis relation: symmetric in time, instantaneous rather than segment-based, qualitative rather than haecceitistic.

Definition 4 · Determinism, relative to an admissible class

Det(L, C)  ⟺  ∀ h₁ h₂ . L(h₁) ∧ L(h₂) ∧ C(h₁) ∧ C(h₂) ∧ Meet(h₁,h₂) ⟹ h₁ = h₂

The class C is the δ coordinate. Instantiate it at Total or at Maximal(L), where a history is maximal iff it is possible and no possible history properly extends it.

Definition 5 · The central object

ℋ(L, C, t, s)  =  { h : L(h) ∧ C(h) ∧ h(t) = s }

and σ acts by pushforward: for r : S → S′, (r∗L)(h′) ⟺ ∃h . L(h) ∧ h′ = r ∘ h. The coarsening operation, the hole-argument quotient and the Everettian branch projection are all instances.

The bridge between the two idioms holds at every δ, and is the only wholly routine result here.

Proposition 1 LEAN

Det(L, C) iff for every t and s, |ℋ(L, C, t, s)| ≤ 1.

And the claim the free-will literature treats as the crux is, as has been observed before and is worth restating precisely, a definitional unfolding:

Proposition 2 LEAN

If |ℋ(L,C,t,s)| = 1 and H ∈ ℋ, there is no H′ ≠ H in ℋ.

Proposition 2 is empty of physical content, and the reason is not that it needs no axioms — plenty of substantive theorems need none, and 28 of the 39 in this development do. CORR The reason is that it unfolds "exactly one". The interesting consequence is not that the inference is trivial but where the content went: into the antecedent, and therefore into the coordinates the antecedent is indexed to. That is the subject of the rest of the paper.

3The coordinates

Each coordinate below is introduced with the dispute that is, on the present analysis, a dispute about it. None of the three is original to this paper; the claim is about their joint structure.

σ — individuation

A redescription r : S → S′. Earman and Norton [16] argued that if a metric and its diffeomorphic image count as distinct possibilities, general relativity is indeterministic — "no matter how small the neighbourhood," in their words. The standard reply denies the antecedent: the physical state is the isometry class. That reply is a choice of σ, and the formalism does not force it.

δ — temporal domain

Whether a history must be defined at every time. The solution of dx/dt = x², x(0)=1 is 1/(1−t): unique wherever defined, defined only on (−∞,1). Under δ = total there is no history at all and |ℋ| = 0; under δ = maximal there is exactly one and |ℋ| = 1. Same equation, same data, opposite verdicts about whether a future exists.

μ — admissibility

Which models are models of the theory. "Classical mechanics is deterministic" quantifies over a class, and Norton's dome is a dispute about the class boundary. Fletcher [18] argues that every principled way of excluding the dome also excludes systems nobody wants to exclude; Antoszek [2] argues that the determinism predicate should not be applied to a framework at all, only to particular theories within it. Both are μ-arguments.

4Independence

The framework is worth having only if the coordinates are genuinely separate. They are. For each, a law and a pair of index-assignments differing in that coordinate alone, with opposite verdicts.

Theorem 3 · δ is not eliminable LEAN

Let blowup be the law whose sole possible history is defined on {0,1,2} and nowhere after — the formal shadow of finite-time blow-up. Then ℋ is empty under δ = total and a singleton under δ = maximal, with σ and μ held fixed.

delta_matters, blowup_no_total_history, blowup_exactlyOne_maximal — no axioms

Theorem 4 · σ can manufacture determinism LEAN

A law with two histories that share a state at time 0 and diverge after is indeterministic. Coarsening by a map that identifies the two divergent successors — and only those — makes the pushforward deterministic.

sigma_can_create — propext, Quot.sound

Theorem 5 · σ can destroy determinism LEAN

A law with two histories that share no state is deterministic, vacuously. Coarsening by a map that identifies their two initial states while keeping their successors apart makes the pushforward indeterministic.

sigma_can_destroy — propext

Theorem 6 · μ is not eliminable LEAN

Two admissibility classes, one properly contained in the other, with the law and the individuation held fixed: every law in the narrow class is deterministic, and the wide class contains one that is not.

mu_matters — propext

Independence claims are cheap if the coordinates never interact. They do interact, and the interaction is the sharpest form of the thesis.

Theorem 7 · The coordinates interact LEAN

On blowup, both δ settings return the verdict "deterministic" — and they disagree completely about whether anything is possible at all. Under δ = total the modal collapse of Proposition 8 fails: □ is vacuously true, ◇ is false, and there is nothing the future must be like because there is no future. Under δ = maximal the collapse holds.

So the word "deterministic" is not even truth-apt without its index, and an index is not recoverable from the verdict.

sigma_delta_interact, modal_collapse_fails_under_total, coordinates_independent — no axioms

Proposition 8 · Modal collapse, correctly relativised LEAN

If |ℋ(L,C,t,s)| = 1 then ◇A ↔ □A for every A. Uniqueness alone gives only ◇ → □; the converse needs existence. The full collapse is exactly |ℋ| = 1, never |ℋ| ≤ 1 — and by Theorem 3, whether |ℋ| = 1 holds is a δ-question.

5σ is non-monotone, and the usual statement of this is backwards

Theorems 4 and 5 together say that coarse-graining is not monotone on determinism in either direction. Both witnesses are non-degenerate — neither collapses the state space to a point, which is the only case the cheap version of this observation covers, and which the previous draft of this paper relied on. CORR

The direction that matters physically is Theorem 5, and it is the direction usually stated the wrong way round. It is tempting to say that coarsening manufactures determinism — identify enough states and everything becomes deterministic — and to infer that fine-graining is the road to indeterminism. The generic situation is the opposite. Ornstein and Weiss [28] survey a body of results showing that a wide class of deterministic systems from classical dynamics are measure-theoretically isomorphic to Bernoulli shifts, so that, in their words, isomorphism theory shows "in many cases the same system can be produced by Newton's laws or by a random mechanism based on coin flipping" (§1). SOURCED Werndl [34] develops the philosophical consequence: certain deterministic and indeterministic descriptions are observationally equivalent at every finite observation level. SOURCED Coarse-graining a deterministic system is precisely how one manufactures a stochastic-looking description; and adding hidden variables — refining — is the standard route by which determinism is restored.

Theorem 5 is the finite, purely logical core of that phenomenon: no measure theory, no mixing, no ergodicity. Two histories that never meet, coarsened so that they meet once and part.

Consequence for Verdict-style claims

If σ is non-monotone in both directions, then no argument of the form "at a fine enough / coarse enough description the world is deterministic" is valid without an independent argument that the description in question is the physically correct one. That is a substantive commitment about individuation, not a technical preliminary — and it is exactly what the hole-argument literature has been arguing about.

6Everettian level-relativity is a σ-shift

Everettian quantum mechanics is routinely described as showing that "one history or many" depends on the level of description — one universal history evolving unitarily, many branch histories emerging through decoherence. This is correct, and it is usually presented as a phenomenon of its own. It is not.

A universal state is a function from branch labels to local states. A branch-relative history is that function evaluated at a label. Evaluation is a map between state spaces. So the branch history is literally the pushforward of the universal history along a σ-redescription — the same operation, and the same coordinate, that the hole argument turns on.

Theorem 9 · The level shift is a σ-shift LEAN

For each branch label b, the branch history is the image of the universal history under push(L, evalb). At the universal level |ℋ| = 1; the branch histories are distinct. Both are true, and they are the same law read at two σ settings.

everett_is_a_sigma_shift, everett_universal_unique, everett_branches_differ

This is a unification, not a deflation. Everett's own 1957 framing keeps only the deterministic process — "the special postulates in the old theory which deal with observation are omitted in the new theory" SOURCED [17] — and nothing here disturbs that. What it disturbs is the idea that the Everettian "levels" puzzle needs its own machinery. It needs an answer to the σ question, which is the question the substantivalism debate has been asking in another vocabulary.

One consequence worth recording, because it cuts against a natural move: the branch decomposition is not unique, and choosing it is itself a σ-choice. Dowker and Kent [11] showed, for the consistent-histories formalism, that the consistency condition permits enormously many mutually incompatible sets of histories, with no formal principle selecting one. That is the same difficulty in the same place. SOURCED

7δ: whether existence fails is a convention, not a discovery

|ℋ| = 1 is a conjunction — at least one, at most one — and determinism as standardly defined delivers only the second conjunct. So far this is familiar; Earman [12] makes the point at length, and the space-invader scenarios are its best-known illustration.

What is not familiar, and what the previous draft of this paper got wrong, is the modal status of the failure. CORR That draft treated "physics fails the existence half far more often than the uniqueness half" as a finding about physics. It is not. It is a consequence of stipulating that histories are total, and under the maximal-solution convention — which is what working mathematicians use — it disappears:

dx/dt = x²,  x(0) = 1  ⟹  x(t) = 1/(1−t)  on  (−∞, 1) verified symbolically · δ = total: |ℋ| = 0  ·  δ = maximal: |ℋ| = 1

Picard–Lindelöf returns exactly one maximal solution through each point for every locally Lipschitz field. So under δ = maximal, the entire class of blow-up phenomena that is supposed to refute determinism instead confirms it. The choice between conventions is not forced by the mathematics, and it is not innocent: it determines whether singularities in general relativity, escape to infinity in the n-body problem, and finite-time blow-up in ordinary differential equations are failures of determinism or instances of it.

A third setting of δ deserves naming, though nothing here turns on it: in canonical quantum gravity the Wheeler–DeWitt constraint leaves no time parameter in the fundamental description at all [22], so the class of admissible histories is not merely restricted but undefined. A framework indexed to δ at least makes that a recognisable limiting case rather than an anomaly.

Two further points of order in this vicinity, both corrections. CORR

Local uniqueness does propagate. The previous draft asserted that in continuous time local uniqueness fails to give global uniqueness, offering Norton's dome as the counterexample. Both halves are wrong. If uniqueness holds locally at every point then it holds globally on a connected interval, by a standard argument: the agreement set of two solutions is non-empty, closed by continuity, and open by local uniqueness, hence everything. And the dome is not a counterexample to that implication, because the dome fails local uniqueness at the apex — it is a case where the hypothesis fails. The genuine discrete/continuous disanalogy is different and is worth stating correctly: in discrete time a step function forces forward uniqueness; in continuous time a well-defined right-hand side does not. What fails to propagate from local to global in continuous time is existence, which is the δ point above.

Non-Lipschitz does not mean non-unique, but "unique" must be indexed to a direction. The equation dx/dt = −x1/3 is the standard example of forward uniqueness without Lipschitz continuity. Under the two-directional agreement relation fixed in Definition 3, however, it is indeterministic: for every T, the solution ((2/3)(T−t))3/2 for t ≤ T, zero after, meets the zero solution at every instant past T and differs from it before. Verified symbolically. The example therefore separates the two settings of the fourth coordinate, and the previous draft used it under the wrong one.

8μ: the dome, and why more mathematics will not settle it

Norton's dome [27] is the cleanest known failure of uniqueness inside Newtonian mechanics. A mass slides frictionlessly on a dome whose height below the apex, as a function of arc length r, is h(r) = (2/3g)r3/2, so that the tangential acceleration is r̈ = √r. At rest at the apex it admits the trivial solution and, for every T ≥ 0, the family r(t) = (t−T)⁴/144 for t ≥ T. The join is C³, not merely C² as usually reported; the mechanism is the failure of Lipschitz continuity of √r at the origin. All checked symbolically here rather than taken on authority.

The philosophical dispute is a μ-dispute, and the participants say so. Malament [24] declines both labels explicitly: the breakdown is not adequately captured "by saying either 'Newtonian particle mechanics is an indeterministic theory' (full stop) or 'Norton's example is not a well-defined Newtonian system' (full stop)," because "we do not have a sufficiently clear idea in the first place what should count as a 'Newtonian system'" (p. 816). SOURCED Fletcher [18] shows that each objection that would rule the dome out "is too strong, in the sense that it also rules out as illegitimate examples of systems that are otherwise unproblematic" (§5), and concludes pluralistically. SOURCED Antoszek [2] argues the predicate should attach to specific theories rather than to the framework.

On the present analysis this is not an impasse but a correctly identified coordinate dispute. Theorem 6 says μ is not eliminable; Malament's "it depends" is the right answer; and the reason no further calculation helps is that there is no calculation to do. The dome's mathematics has been settled since 2003.

10Quantum mechanics, and a correction

Textbook quantum mechanics contains two dynamical rules — unitary evolution, and collapse on measurement with Born-rule weights — of which only the second is stochastic, and only the second is stated in terms of an undefined primitive. Determinism is therefore downstream of the measurement problem, not upstream of it, and the candidate resolutions divide: Bohmian mechanics and Everettian quantum mechanics are deterministic at the fundamental level, objective-collapse theories are not.

Bell's theorem does not bear on this in the direction usually supposed. In the 1964 argument [3] determinism is derived from locality and the perfect anticorrelations rather than assumed; by 1971 Bell had already noted in a footnote that his formalism is "appropriate also for indeterminism with a certain local character," calling his own 1964 retreat to the deterministic representation "quite arbitrary"; and the 1976 local-causality formulation is probabilistic from the outset, with factorization derived. SOURCED [4,5] What Bell's theorem does is constrain: a deterministic theory must be non-local or must violate statistical independence. That is informative, and the previous draft's abstract claimed instead that Bell "constrains none of this," contradicting its own §10. CORR

10.1 Linearity does not immunise the Schrödinger equation

The previous draft asserted that "considered by itself, the Schrödinger equation is more robustly deterministic than Newton's laws: it is linear, so none of the [classical] pathologies arise." This is false, and the correction is not a detail — it is the place where the δ coordinate reappears inside quantum mechanics. CORR

Stone's theorem requires self-adjointness, not symmetry. A Hamiltonian that is symmetric but not essentially self-adjoint admits a family of distinct self-adjoint extensions, each generating a different unitary group. Same initial state, same formal Hamiltonian, many evolutions: a failure of uniqueness inside ordinary quantum mechanics, requiring no collapse postulate and no interpretation.

And the failure is not sporadic. It occurs, by a correspondence going back to Wightman, precisely where the classical particle escapes to infinity in finite time. For V(x) = −x⁴ the classical escape time from x₀ at zero energy is √(m/2)/x₀ < ∞ — verified symbolically here — and the corresponding Hamiltonian fails to be essentially self-adjoint, so the quantum dynamics is fixed only by an additional boundary condition at infinity. Earman has developed exactly this point [14,15]; Reed and Simon [30] is the standard technical source.

Why this matters for the framework

The classical and quantum pathologies are the same pathology, and it is a δ-phenomenon in both: the dynamics runs out of domain, and a convention must be supplied to say what happens then. In the classical case the convention is total-versus-maximal; in the quantum case it is the choice of self-adjoint extension. Treating the two as unrelated — one a curiosity of singular potentials, the other a technicality of operator theory — is what allowed the false claim to look plausible.

11General relativity

General relativity has a genuine initial-value formulation, and it is the strongest determinism result in physics. Choquet-Bruhat and Geroch [8] prove that an initial data set has a development extending every other, unique up to isometry. SOURCED The force of that depends on a definition given earlier in their paper: a development requires the data surface to be a Cauchy surface, so "every other development" already means every other globally hyperbolic one. As Sbierski [31] observes, restricting to globally hyperbolic developments is unavoidable if one wants any global uniqueness result at all. SOURCED Determinism here is partly assumed in the vocabulary, not wholly derived.

The theorem delivers uniqueness up to isometry. Whether that is determinism is the σ question, and it is live. Weatherall [33] argued that the hole argument rests on comparing isometric models by a map that is not an isometry, which mathematical practice does not license. Halvorson and Manchak [20] pressed further with a rigidity result — isometries agreeing on any non-empty open set agree everywhere, so there are no hole isometries — and concluded that the mathematical claim supporting the argument is false. SOURCED

The previous draft reported that result as settling the matter. It does not, and the objection is directly on the inference this paper cares about. CORR Dewar [10] argues that Halvorson and Manchak engage a modified argument rather than the traditional one, and — the load-bearing point — that rigidity does not entail determinism. On the present framework that is exactly right and should have been predictable: rigidity is a fact about the automorphism structure at a fixed σ, and the dispute is about which σ to fix. A theorem proved inside one coordinate setting cannot adjudicate between settings.

That a 2025 paper is still fixing the definition of determinism for general relativity [21,25] is the relevant datum. It is not a sign that the field is confused; it is what a coordinate dispute looks like from inside.

12Agency: what the indexing changes, and what it does not

The argument from determinism to the impossibility of alternative action has three premises, not two, and the third is usually left unstated.

Theorem 10 · The supervenience premise LEAN

Let an ascription be a function of the history, a further parameter, and a time; let it supervene iff the further parameter makes no difference. Then: (i) given supervenience, |ℋ| = 1 fixes the action; (ii) there is a law with exactly one possible history, a non-supervenient ascription, and two distinct actions on that one history.

So the step from one history to one action requires causal closure of the physical as a separate premise.

unique_history_fixes_action, action_not_fixed_without_supervenience

This replaces a theorem in the previous draft which carried the same name and proved only that a function of two arguments may depend on its second — no content at all. CORR The point is philosophically unremarkable and independently well supported; what matters is that it is a premise.

Three further things hold, and none is changed by the indexing. Determinism forecloses only one reading of "could have done otherwise." Same prior state and same laws is foreclosed; different prior state, same laws survives, because determinism constrains how histories may relate and not how many there are. Indeterminism rescues nothing — the rollback objection [32]. An uncaused deviation is not an exercise of control; it is a deviation that happens to the agent. And the conditional is analytic: if leeway libertarianism is defined as requiring alternatives under an identical past, then |ℋ| = 1 excludes it by construction.

What the indexing adds is a constraint on the debate's form. "Determinism threatens free will" is not a proposition until σ, δ and μ are fixed, and the fixing is not neutral between the parties. An agent's action plausibly supervenes on a coarse description of the world; by Theorem 5, determinism at a coarse description is not entailed by determinism at a fine one, in either direction. So the compatibilist and the incompatibilist may each be right about their own coordinate assignment while disagreeing verbally about a single English sentence. Lewis's reply to the Consequence Argument [23] — that one may be able to do something such that, were one to do it, a law would have been broken, without being able to break a law — is a dispute of exactly this shape, conducted over the modal semantics rather than over any physics.

Note also the honest limit: none of this is an argument that physics settles the free-will question. On the two questions that would matter — whether alternative possibilities are required for responsibility [19], and whether sourcehood survives determinism or chance [29] — the present framework is silent, because those are questions about the analysis of agency.

13Relation to prior work, stated plainly

A framework paper is worth little if it repackages known results, so the boundary should be explicit.

Not new here. That determinism admits several inequivalent definitions: Montague, Lewis, Belot's D1–D3, Müller and Placek [26], Halvorson, Manchak and Weatherall [21]. That determinism is sensitive to how states are individuated: Earman and Norton [16], Belot [6], Butterfield [7], and the whole hole-argument literature. That the model class is not fixed by the formalism: Fletcher [18], Antoszek [2]. That coarse-graining can make deterministic systems look stochastic: Ornstein and Weiss [28], Werndl [34]. That |ℋ| = 1 factors into existence and uniqueness, and that physics can fail existence: Earman [12], and Earman's own later survey [13], which covers most of the territory of §§7–11 of this paper and should be the first stop for any reader.

New here, as far as I have been able to establish. (i) That these are separable coordinates of one predicate rather than competing diagnoses of one puzzle. (ii) The independence theorem: Theorems 3–7, with machine-checked countermodels, showing that no coordinate is eliminable and that they interact. (iii) The non-monotonicity of σ in both directions with non-degenerate witnesses — Theorem 5 in particular, the coarsening that destroys determinism, which is the direction usually stated backwards. (iv) The identification of Everettian level-relativity with the σ coordinate (Theorem 9), which makes the branching puzzle and the substantivalism puzzle the same puzzle. (v) The diagnostic map of §9 and the prediction it carries.

Novelty risk, stated rather than hidden. Each coordinate individually is discussed in the literature above, and I cannot exclude that the joint framework or something close to it appears somewhere I have not read — the determinism literature is large and much of it is in books I have not opened. The independence results I am confident are not in the sources cited here. A referee who knows of a precedent should treat claim (i) as the one most likely to fall.

  2. §7 — false. The claim that in continuous time local uniqueness does not yield global uniqueness, with Norton's dome as counterexample. Local uniqueness does propagate, by connectedness; the dome fails local uniqueness.
  3. §7 — unjustified. "Physics fails the existence half far more often than the uniqueness half" was presented as a finding about physics. It is a consequence of stipulating total histories, and it is now the δ coordinate.
  4. §7 — inconsistent. dx/dt = −x1/3 was offered as deterministic. Under the paper's own two-directional definition it is not.
  5. Axiom audit — transposed. The previous draft reported 10 axiom-free and 16 propext/Quot.sound results; its own code printed 16 and 10. The present development prints 28 and 11 and audits every theorem in the file, which the previous one did not.
  6. Two theorems renamed or replaced. unique_chance_does_not_give_unique_history took a "chance function" that was an unused parameter typed histories → ℕ, with no normalisation; it proved only that a two-history law has two histories. alternative_action_without_supervenience proved that a function of two arguments may depend on its second. Both are replaced by statements that say what their names claim (Theorem 10).
  7. Verification labels. The previous draft marked as machine-verified several claims its code did not check — notably a claim about chaos backed by a SymPy check on x′ = ax, a linear ODE that is not a chaotic system. The checks now use the tent and logistic maps, with the conjugacy and the Lyapunov exponent verified.
  8. Hypothesis ranking — category error. The previous draft ranked "conditional determinism" as best supported against three rivals. That claim is about our evidential state; its rivals are about the world. The correct statement, which it also made, is that the rival hypotheses are not discriminated by any experiment performed to date.
  9. Coarsening. The abstract claimed coarsening manufactures determinism and refining destroys it. Only the degenerate half was proved, and the generic direction is the reverse (§5).
  10. Literature. Earman [14,15], Weatherall [33], Dewar [10], Müller and Placek [26], Antoszek [2] and Dowker and Kent [11] were absent or uncited; each bears directly on a claim made.

15Limitations, and what would refute this

The framework is discrete and finite. The Lean development uses ℕ time and finite state spaces. That is adequate for independence results — a countermodel is a countermodel — but it means the framework itself has not been shown to interact correctly with the analytic structure of real physical theories. The continuous cases in §§7–8 and §10 are verified separately in SymPy, not integrated.

The fourth coordinate is held fixed by fiat. Every result above is stated at the Montague/Lewis agreement relation. Whether the independence of σ, δ and μ survives at, say, a segment-based or haecceitistic relation is open, and I expect at least the δ results to be sensitive to it.

The diagnostic map is an interpretive claim, not a theorem. §9 asserts that specific historical disputes sit on specific coordinates. That is an argument about what those authors are disagreeing about, and it can be wrong dispute by dispute without touching Theorems 3–10.

What would refute the central thesis. A demonstration that one of σ, δ, μ is definable from the others — which the independence theorems block only for the specific agreement relation fixed here. Or a principled argument that one coordinate has a canonical setting forced by physics rather than chosen: the most promising candidate is δ, where a case can be made that maximality is simply correct and totality a confusion. If that case succeeds, §7 becomes a two-line remark and the framework loses a third of its content — and I would regard that as a good outcome, since it would settle a dispute rather than classify it.

-- Determinism relative to a class C of admissible histories.
-- C IS the delta coordinate: instantiate at Total or at Maximal L.
def DetIn {S : Type} (L : Law S) (C : PHist S → Prop) : Prop :=
  ∀ h₁ h₂, L.Possible h₁ → L.Possible h₂ → C h₁ → C h₂ → MeetAt h₁ h₂ → h₁ = h₂

-- Theorem 5: coarsening DESTROYS determinism. rSplit identifies the two
-- initial states and keeps the successors apart, so two histories that
-- never met now meet once and part. Non-degenerate: rSplit is not constant.
theorem sigma_can_destroy :
    DetIn disjoint Total ∧ ¬ DetIn (push disjoint rSplit) Total := by
  refine ⟨disjoint_det, ?_⟩
  intro hd
  have p₁ : (push disjoint rSplit).Possible (fun n => (hBC n).map rSplit) :=
    ⟨hBC, Or.inl rfl, rfl⟩
  have p₂ : (push disjoint rSplit).Possible (fun n => (hAD' n).map rSplit) :=
    ⟨hAD', Or.inr rfl, rfl⟩
  have e := hd _ _ p₁ p₂ t₁ t₂ ⟨0, S3.x, by simp [hBC, rSplit], by simp [hAD', rSplit]⟩
  have := congrFun e 1
  simp [hBC, hAD', rSplit] at this
