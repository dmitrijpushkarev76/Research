Linearity Does Not Immunise

The Schrödinger equation is linear, so it has none of the pathologies of classical mechanics. That is false, and the classical and quantum failures sit at the same threshold — but they are not the same failure.

Retraction, and what this note replaces

This note replaces Determinism Is an Indexed Predicate (Revision 2, 12 September 2026), whose central claims are withdrawn. That paper argued that a determinism verdict is a function of a law plus three independent, non-eliminable coordinates. A referee refuted two of its three headline claims with counter-theorems stated against its own definitions, and I have compiled all of them. §6 gives the full record; the counter-development is in the repository as lean/Referee.lean.

What survives is smaller, is not new, and is set out below: a correction to a claim about quantum mechanics that the withdrawn paper made and then misclassified, three corrections to claims about ordinary differential equations, and one small sharpening of the classical side of a correspondence due to Wightman and developed by Earman. Nothing here is presented as a discovery. The note exists because the corrected claims are common and the corrections are checkable.

Summary

A familiar thought: the pathologies of classical mechanics — Norton's dome, escape to infinity, finite-time blow-up — arise because the equations are non-linear, and the Schrödinger equation is linear, so quantum mechanics is free of them. The thought is wrong. Stone's theorem requires self-adjointness, not symmetry, and a Hamiltonian that is symmetric but not essentially self-adjoint generates not one unitary group but a family of them, one per self-adjoint extension. Same initial state, same formal Hamiltonian, many evolutions.

The threshold at which this happens is the classical escape threshold. For V(x) = −xn at zero energy the classical particle reaches infinity in finite time exactly when n > 2 — verified symbolically here, and sharp at the harmonic case — and it is at that same threshold that essential self-adjointness fails.

But the two failures are not identical, and the withdrawn paper's claim that they are "the same failure" was a pun on two senses of domain. Classically the solution runs out of time: an existence failure. Quantum-mechanically every self-adjoint extension generates a unitary group defined for all t ∈ ℝ; what is underdetermined is the operator, through a boundary condition at infinity. That is a uniqueness failure, and it is resolved by deciding which operator is the theory. Correlated failures at a shared threshold, not one failure.

1The claim, and why it is tempting

Classical mechanics has a well-catalogued set of determinism failures. Norton's dome gives non-uniqueness from a non-Lipschitz force; dx/dt = x² gives finite-time blow-up; the n-body problem gives non-collision singularities, and time-reversed, space invaders. Every one of these involves a non-linearity somewhere, which makes the following inference look safe:

The Schrödinger equation is linear. Linear equations have global unique solutions. Therefore unitary quantum mechanics is deterministic in a way classical mechanics is not, and all quantum indeterminism is in the collapse postulate.

The last clause is right. The inference to it is not. The step that fails is "linear equations have global unique solutions," which is true for bounded operators and false in general for the unbounded ones that physical Hamiltonians are.

2Stone's theorem needs self-adjointness

Stone's theorem gives a bijection between strongly continuous one-parameter unitary groups and self-adjoint operators. Self-adjointness is H = H† including equality of domains, which is strictly stronger than symmetry (⟨Hψ, φ⟩ = ⟨ψ, Hφ⟩ on a common dense domain).

A symmetric operator defined on a natural domain such as C0∞ may have many self-adjoint extensions, or none. Where there are many, each generates a different unitary group, and the Schrödinger equation alone does not say which one is the dynamics. This is not an artefact of a bad domain choice; it is the statement that the formal differential expression underdetermines the operator. SOURCED [8]

The consequence

For such a Hamiltonian there is a one-parameter state Ψ(0) and a family of evolutions {Uα(t)Ψ(0)}, all satisfying the Schrödinger equation, all unitary, all defined for every time. No collapse postulate is involved, and no interpretation of quantum mechanics is involved. Uniqueness fails inside the part of the formalism everyone agrees is fundamental.

This is Earman's point, made at length in two 2008 papers [2,3] and in his handbook survey [1]. It is not mine, and the reader who wants the full treatment should go there rather than here. What this note adds is one small sharpening of the classical half, in §3, and a correction to how the correspondence is usually described, in §4.

3The classical threshold, and that it is sharp

The correspondence, due to Wightman and discussed by Earman [2], is that essential self-adjointness fails where the classical particle escapes to infinity in finite time. The classical half is elementary and worth computing, because it makes the threshold visible.

Take V(x) = −xn and energy E = 0. Conservation gives ½mẋ² = xn, so ẋ = xn/2√(2/m), and the time to reach infinity from x₀ > 0 is

τ(x₀)  =  √(m/2)  ∫x₀∞ x−n/2 dx   < ∞   ⟺   n > 2 verified symbolically for n = 1, 2, 3, 4, 6 · finite exactly at n = 3, 4, 6

The threshold is sharp at the harmonic case. At n = 2 the integral diverges and the particle never arrives; at any n > 2 it arrives in finite time. For n = 4 the escape time is √(m/2)/x₀ exactly. CHECKED

So the inverted harmonic oscillator sits precisely on the boundary, which is the right way to see why −x⁴ is the textbook case and −x² is not. The quantum statement matching this — that −d²/dx² − x⁴ on C0∞(ℝ) is not essentially self-adjoint, by the Weyl limit-point/limit-circle criterion — I have not verified and take from Reed and Simon [8]. NOT OPENED

4The same threshold, two different failures

Here is the correction that matters, and it is a correction to the withdrawn paper as much as to anyone.

Classical, V = −x⁴Quantum, H = −d²/dx² − x⁴
What runs outThe solution's interval of timeNothing. Every extension gives a group on all of ℝ
Failure typeExistenceUniqueness
Repaired byA convention: total vs. maximal solutionsA physical choice: which self-adjoint extension
ResidueNo history past the singular timeMany histories, all total, all unitary

The withdrawn paper said "the classical and quantum failures are the same failure," and classified both as a question about the temporal domain. That is wrong twice over. The quantum failure does not touch the temporal domain at all — the histories are total — and it is a uniqueness failure, which the same paper spent a section insisting must be held apart from existence failures. The word doing the illicit work is domain: an interval of time in the classical case, an operator domain in a Hilbert space in the quantum case. CORRECTED

What is true, and is the whole content of the correspondence, is that the same classical incompleteness is diagnostic of both. A potential steep enough to let a particle escape in finite time is a potential steep enough to leave the quantum dynamics underdetermined at infinity. Correlated, at a shared and sharp threshold — not identical.

5Three corrections about ordinary differential equations

These are elementary, and all three were stated wrongly in one or other draft of the withdrawn paper. They are collected because each is a claim one meets in informal discussion.

5.1 Local uniqueness does propagate

It is sometimes said that in continuous time, uniqueness holding locally at every point does not give uniqueness globally, and that Norton's dome shows this. Both halves are wrong.

If uniqueness holds locally at every point, it holds globally on a connected interval. Let x₁, x₂ be solutions on a common interval I agreeing at t₀, and let A = {t ∈ I : x₁(t) = x₂(t)}. Then A is non-empty, closed in I by continuity, and open in I by local uniqueness; since I is connected, A = I.

And the dome is not a counterexample to that implication, because the dome fails local uniqueness at the apex — it is a case where the hypothesis does not hold. The real discrete/continuous disanalogy is different: in discrete time a step function forces forward uniqueness, whereas in continuous time a well-defined right-hand side does not. What fails to propagate from local to global in continuous time is existence, not uniqueness.

5.2 "Forward-unique" is not "deterministic"

The equation dx/dt = −x1/3 is the standard example of uniqueness without Lipschitz continuity. Forward in time it is unique: the origin attracts from both sides. Backward it is not. For every T,

x(t) = ((2/3)(T − t))3/2 for t ≤ T,  0 for t > T verified: dx/dw = x1/3 with w = T − t > 0; meets x ≡ 0 at T with matching slope

is a solution agreeing with the zero solution at every instant after T and differing from it before. Under any definition on which agreement at one instant forces agreement at all instants — Montague's, Lewis's, and the usual possible-worlds formulations [5] — this law is indeterministic. So the example shows Lipschitz continuity is unnecessary for forward uniqueness only, and citing it as showing a non-Lipschitz law can be deterministic requires saying which direction is meant. CHECKED

5.3 The dome's join is C³

Norton's dome [6] has h(r) = (2/3g)r3/2, hence r̈ = √r, and the non-trivial solutions r(t) = (t−T)⁴/144 for t ≥ T. The join with the rest solution is routinely described as C², including by Malament [4]. Differentiating at T gives [0, 0, 0, 0, 1/6] for orders 0 through 4: the join is exactly C³, one degree smoother than usually stated, with the fourth derivative the first to survive. CHECKED

Nothing turns on this — C² is true, being implied by C³ — but the sharp value is the one to quote.

6The retraction in full

The withdrawn paper claimed that determinism is a predicate of a law plus three independent coordinates: σ (how states are individuated), δ (whether histories must be total in time), μ (which models count). Three claims are withdrawn.

Withdrawn 1 · The coordinates are not independent, and two are eliminable

Folding the admissibility class into the law gives DetIn L C ↔ DetIn (restrict L C) triv, where restrict L C := ⟨fun h => L.Possible h ∧ C h⟩. This compiles from the paper's own definitions with no axioms, and the same move eliminates μ. And σ was never a separate slot, since push L r is itself a law. Composing, every (law, σ, δ) verdict is an unindexed verdict about one constructed law.

There is one slot — which set of histories you are talking about — and σ, δ, μ are three motivations for filling it, not three coordinates of a predicate. The abstract's claim that "no definition of determinism that suppresses any of them is well-formed" is false.

Withdrawn 2 · The independence theorem conflates two predicates

Its δ conjunct is about ExactlyOne (an existence claim); its σ and μ conjuncts are about DetIn. On the paper's own δ witness the determinism verdict does not move — the paper proves this itself and does not reconcile it with the abstract.

This one is repairable, and I have repaired it: a law with two mutually non-extending partial histories meeting at one instant, plus a total history meeting neither, is deterministic under the total convention and indeterministic under the maximal one. So δ can flip a determinism verdict. The paper simply never proved it. The repair does not rescue Withdrawn 1.

Withdrawn 3 · Everettian branching is not a σ-shift

The pushforward of a singleton law is a singleton law, so the paper's Everett model is deterministic at every σ setting: the branch multiplicity the theorem exists to explain never arises in it. And the law whose possible histories are the branch histories is not the pushforward of the universal law along any redescription — pushing forward along one map cannot turn one history into many. Branching would need the image under a family of maps, which is a different operation and is not the hole-argument quotient.

Two further defects are worth recording because they are defects of method rather than of content. The theorem named sigma_delta_interact, which the paper called the sharpest form of its thesis, contains no σ in its statement; the σ claim lived only in a code comment. And four of the paper's seventeen symbolic checks verified nothing: one had the literal True as its predicate, and the "tent map" check only ever differentiated 2x, never constructing the map — the identical check passes for x ↦ 2x + 7, which is not chaotic. Both are the exact defect class the paper's own corrigenda section claimed to have purged from its predecessor.

The verification file accompanying this note audits its own source and fails if any check's predicate is a literal constant.

What of the withdrawn paper survives

  • The §10.1 correction on essential self-adjointness — restated here, with its classification fixed.
  • The local-to-global uniqueness correction — §5.1 here.
  • The −x1/3 observation — §5.2 here.
  • That coarsening a state space can destroy determinism. True, but standard: it is the failure of lumpability, and requires a partition invariant under the dynamics to avoid. The paper attributed it to Ornstein and Weiss [7], which is a misuse — their isomorphism results use generating partitions and preserve determinism; what they establish is statistical indistinguishability, not failure of uniqueness. Werndl [9] is the right citation for the philosophical claim.

7What this note is not

It is not novel. The central point is Earman's [1,2,3]; the classical threshold computation is undergraduate mechanics; the ODE corrections are textbook. The one thing I have not found stated in the sources I could reach is the sharpness observation of §3 — that the escape criterion sits exactly at the harmonic case — and I would expect that to be somewhere in the literature too.

It is also not a survey of determinism. Earman's handbook chapter [1] is that, covers everything here and much more, and should be read first.

What the note is for: the claim corrected in §§2–4 is repeated often enough, including by me a day ago, that a short checkable statement of why it is wrong seems worth having.

After the previous version shipped a check whose predicate was the constant True, a value-level guard is not enough — a genuine computation may legitimately evaluate to True — so the audit walks the file's own AST and fails the run if any check() call has a literal constant in its predicate position:

def audit_own_source():
    tree = ast.parse(open(__file__).read())
    bad, n = [], 0
    for node in ast.walk(tree):
        if isinstance(node, ast.Call) and getattr(node.func, "id", None) == "check":
            n += 1
            if isinstance(node.args[1], ast.Constant):
                bad.append((node.lineno, ast.unparse(node.args[1])))
    return n, bad
